## Overview

A private page is visible only to __you__ — the person who owns it. Everyone else is refused, including administrators. Your private pages live in your own private area, and nobody else's account can read, list, or search them.

Your private area is yours alone. Nothing reaches into it because of someone's role.

### Key facts at a glance

%%table-striped
|| Property || Value ||
| Access | The page's owner only |
| Administrators | No access — the same refusal as any other user |
| Search visibility | Only the owner sees the page in search and in listings |
| Files | Files you upload to a private page are private too, and belong to you |
| Required pages | Cannot be made private |
| Encryption | Files on the server are not encrypted — see [Limitations|#limitations] |
/%

## Marking a Page as Private

Tick the __Private__ checkbox:

- on the __Create New Page__ form, when you create the page, or
- on a page's __Edit__ form, to make an existing page private.

You can also set it in the page's frontmatter:

```yaml
private: true
```

The page moves into your private area when you save. Its name, URL and history stay as they were.

### Who owns the page

The owner is the page's __author__ as first recorded when the page was saved. That record does not change if the `author` field in the frontmatter is edited later, so changing that field cannot hand your page to somebody else — or take it from you.

## Who Can See a Private Page

%%table-striped
|| User type || Can view? || Can edit? || Can delete? || Can view history? ||
| The page's owner | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Administrators | No (403) | No (403) | No (403) | No (403) |
| Other signed-in users | No (403) | No (403) | No (403) | No (403) |
| Anonymous visitors | No (403) | No | No | No |
/%

The [Page Audience] setting and any `access` entries are __ignored__ on a private page. Listing a role or a username there does not let anyone in.

---

## Search Behaviour

A private page appears in search results and page listings __only for its owner__. No title, excerpt or other detail is shown to anyone else.

When you make the page public again and save, it becomes searchable for everyone who may read it.

---

## Files on a Private Page

- A file you upload __onto a private page__ is always private, whether or not you tick the Private box on the upload form. It is stored inside that page's private area and belongs to the page's owner.
- A private file is served __only to its owner__. Anyone else who opens its link gets __403 Forbidden__, whichever page links to it.
- You can also tick __Private__ when uploading with no page, or on a public page. That file goes into your own private area and is yours alone.
- A file that is already public stays public if it is linked from a private page. Linking never moves a file or changes who may read it.
- If you later make the page public, files uploaded while it was private __stay private__. To publish one, upload it again once the page is public.

## Making a Private Page Public Again

- Open the page and click __Edit__.
- Untick the __Private__ checkbox (or set `private: false` in frontmatter).
- Click __Save__.

The page and its history move back to the normal area and become visible to everyone who may read the wiki. Files uploaded while the page was private stay private.

## Limitations

### Required pages cannot be private

Built-in pages that ship with [{$applicationname}] are part of the product and are always readable. Marking one private is refused:

> Required pages cannot be marked as private.

### Sharing with somebody else

A private page is yours alone. There is no way today to let another person read one. Sharing a private area with someone you choose is planned, and until it exists the honest answer is that private means private to you. If you need to share content, put it on a normal page and use [Page Audience] instead.

### Files on the server are not encrypted

Access control is enforced by [{$applicationname}]. The files themselves are stored as ordinary text on the server, so anyone who can read the server's disk or its backups can read them — a system operator, for example. __Do not keep passwords, keys or other secrets on a private page.__ Encryption of a private area is being worked on separately.

### Your version history is private too

Every saved version of a private page is kept inside your private area and follows the same rule: only you can read it.

## Access Denied Behaviour

Anyone who is not the page's owner gets __HTTP 403 Access Denied__:

%%table-striped
|| URL pattern || Action ||
| `/view/{PageName}` | View page |
| `/edit/{PageName}` | Edit page |
| `/history/{PageName}` | View page history |
| `/delete/{PageName}` | Delete page |
| `/attachments/{id}` | Download a file |
/%

The refusal says the person does not have permission, rather than that the page does not exist. The owner can therefore confirm the page is there and that the restriction is working.

## Tips and Best Practices

- __Use private pages for drafts.__ Write privately, then untick Private when you are ready to publish.
- __Files stay private.__ If you publish a page later, upload its files again so they become public with it.
- __Naming.__ Private page names follow the normal rules. Only you see them, so name them for yourself.
- __Nobody is reading over your shoulder.__ Administrators cannot open your private pages, and cannot see them in search or listings.
- __Not for secrets.__ Private pages are hidden from other people, but they are not encrypted on the server. Keep passwords and keys out of them.
