# Using Private Stores

A __private store__ is a place on [{$applicationname}] that belongs to you. Pages and files you keep there are yours alone: nobody else can open them, find them in a search, or see their titles — administrators included.

Your first private page creates your own store, called __default__. Your site may offer others, each for a different purpose, and an encrypted one is set up deliberately — see below.

## Making a page private

Tick __Private__ when you edit a page. The page moves into your default store, and its address changes to show where it now lives:

```text
/private/your-name/default/Shopping list
```

Untick the box and the page goes back to being an ordinary page that everyone can read. Only you can move your own page either way.

Your private pages are listed under __Private Pages__ on your profile. They never appear in the site's page index, recent changes, or anyone else's search.

## Two kinds of store

| | Plain store | Encrypted store |
|---|---|---|
| Who can open it | Only you | Only you |
| How it is protected | By this site refusing everyone else | By encryption, unlocked with your password |
| If someone reads the disk directly | They can read your pages | They see scrambled text only |

A plain store is enough for a shopping list. An encrypted store suits medical notes, financial records, or anything you would mind being read if the server itself were copied.

Whether a store is encrypted is decided when the store is offered to you. You cannot change an existing store from one to the other.

## Creating an encrypted store

Your site decides which stores it offers, and where it puts the link to set one up — often on your profile. Following that link walks you through a short door:

- 1 You are told what the store is for, and that it will be encrypted.
- 2 You type your password again. It protects the new key.
- 3 You are shown __twelve recovery words__.
- 4 You type some of them back, to prove you wrote them down.

The store is only created at the last step. If the words do not match, nothing is created and nothing is kept.

## The twelve words

The words are shown __once__. They are not stored anywhere, and nobody at this site has a copy — not the administrator, not the person who runs the server.

They are the only way back into an encrypted store if you forget your password.

- Write them down before you start, in order, and keep them where you keep important papers.
- Do not keep them only in the store they protect, or only on the device you sign in from.
- If you lose both your password and the words, the contents of that store cannot be read by anyone, ever. There is no recovery.

## Unlocking

An encrypted store opens when you sign in with your password, and closes when you sign out.

If you signed in another way, you will see a notice that your store is locked, with a link to unlock it. Typing your password there opens it for the rest of that visit.

You can also unlock it with your twelve words instead of your password: on the unlock page, choose __Use your 12 recovery words instead__ and type them in order. Capitals and extra spaces do not matter.

While a store is locked, its pages simply are not there: they do not appear in lists or searches, and opening one directly says the page does not exist.

## Linking to a private page

Ordinary links, like `[Shopping list]`, always mean a public page.

To link to a private page, put the store in front of it:

```text
[default/Shopping list]
```

The link works for you and shows the store's name. To anyone else it leads nowhere — it does not tell them whether such a page exists.

While you type `[` in the editor, your own private pages are offered alongside public ones, marked __private__ and showing which store they are in. Choosing one inserts the right link.

## Searching

Search covers your own private pages, including those in an encrypted store, by title and by the words in them. A private result's address begins with `/private/`, which is how you can tell it apart.

Nobody else's search reaches your private pages, whatever they search for.

## Deleting and restoring

Deleting a private page moves it into your own store's trash, with its history.

__My Trash__ on your profile lists what you have deleted, and lets you:

- __Restore__ it — the page comes back where it was, with its history and its place in your search.
- __Delete it for good__ — the page, its history and the record of it are removed and cannot be recovered.

Your site clears trash automatically after a time it sets — thirty days unless an administrator changes it. For an encrypted store that housekeeping happens while you are signed in, because the site cannot open the store otherwise.

Restoring is refused if you have since made another page with the same title in that store. Rename one of them and try again.

## Files

A file you attach to a private page is kept in the same store as the page, and is as private as the page. In an encrypted store the file's contents are encrypted too.

The same file uploaded to a private store and to a public page stays two separate copies, so a private upload never shares a record with a public one.

## What an administrator can see

Nothing in the site shows an administrator what is in your store. They cannot open it, list what is in it, read a page or a file, search it, or restore anything from your trash. Asking for one of your private pages tells them only that the page does not exist — the same answer they get for a page that was never written.

Someone with access to the server's disk is a different matter. In a plain store they can read your pages there; in an encrypted store they find only scrambled text.

An administrator can delete your account, which deletes what you kept.

## Not yet available

Two things people reasonably expect are not built:

- __Downloading your own store__, or including private data in a site backup.
- __Sharing a private store__, or one page in it, with another person.

Until they are, treat an encrypted store as a place you can write and read, but not yet hand to anyone or take with you.

## If something looks wrong

- A page you expected is missing, and your store is encrypted — check the locked notice and unlock it.
- A private link is red — the page does not exist yet in that store. Clicking it opens a new page there.
- A page you made private is still listed somewhere public — tell an administrator, and include the page's title. That should not happen.
