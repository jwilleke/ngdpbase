# Authentication developer guide

What a developer has to know to change how people sign in. Who may do what once signed in is [security-developer-guide.md](security-developer-guide.md); the design history, open plans and YourPHR's needs are in [planning/authentication.md](../planning/authentication.md).

## Provider docs

Every sign-in method is a provider registered with [AuthManager](../managers/AuthManager.md) (the methods, and the one door routes call). Each has its own page:

- Signing people in
  - [PasswordAuthProvider](../providers/PasswordAuthProvider.md) — username and password, checked against the hash on the user record
  - [PasskeyAuthProvider](../providers/PasskeyAuthProvider.md) — passkeys (WebAuthn); a passkey alone signs in at AAL2, phishing-resistant
  - [MagicLinkAuthProvider](../providers/MagicLinkAuthProvider.md) — an emailed single-use sign-in link
  - [OidcAuthProvider](../providers/OidcAuthProvider.md) — sign-in through any outside OpenID Connect provider, by an identity connected to the account: what it may do, the flows and their checks
    - [Google](../providers/OidcAuthProvider-Google.md) — the `google` preset, and registering the site in the Google Cloud console
    - [Microsoft](../providers/OidcAuthProvider-Microsoft.md) — the `microsoft` preset (personal accounts only), and registering the site in Microsoft Entra
    - [Apple](../providers/OidcAuthProvider-Apple.md) — the `apple` preset: a client secret the site signs, the POSTed callback, and registering with Apple
    - [authentik](../providers/OidcAuthProvider-Authentik.md) — a self-hosted authentik as an entry with its own issuer (no preset)
  - [CloudflareAccessAuthProvider](../providers/CloudflareAccessAuthProvider.md) — trusts Cloudflare Access's `cf-access-jwt-assertion` header when the site sits behind it
- Requests from clients (bearer tokens; never a session)
  - [AgentTokenAuthProvider](../providers/AgentTokenAuthProvider.md) — an agent token a person minted, delegating part of their own authority
  - [OidcBearerAuthProvider](../providers/OidcBearerAuthProvider.md) — access tokens from this site's own OpenID Connect server, for apps a person allowed
  - [AuthentikBearerAuthProvider](../providers/AuthentikBearerAuthProvider.md) — an authentik-issued token held by a headless client
- The contract and what sign-in methods keep
  - [BaseAuthProvider](../providers/BaseAuthProvider.md) — the interface every sign-in method implements
  - [BaseCredentialsProvider](../providers/BaseCredentialsProvider.md) and [FileCredentialsProvider](../providers/FileCredentialsProvider.md) — the credentials store: passkeys, verified addresses, connected outside identities, as signed rows
  - [FileOidcAdapter](../providers/FileOidcAdapter.md) — storage for this site's own OpenID Connect server ([OidcManager](../managers/OidcManager.md)): grants, sessions and tokens of apps people sign in to with this site

For people signing in, the shipped help page [Signing In With Another Account](../../required-pages/349c67da-871c-40a5-8581-89bad4988970.md) is the user-facing counterpart of the outside providers: connecting one on the profile, signing in with it, and the email step.

## Standing rules

- __AuthManager is the one door.__ Routes call `AuthManager`, never a provider. Every sign-in method is a registered `AuthProvider` (`registerProvider`), so a new method is a provider, not a route. This covers every check of a secret, not only a sign-in: re-checking a password before a change, an API key, a token. The provider is the connection to its method and owns the check; where the method's data lives (the password hash on the user record) is the storing manager's (operator, 2026-10-11).
- __What is offered is configuration.__ `ngdpbase.auth.factors` lists the methods a site offers, one entry per provider: `authproviderid`, `primary` (can start a sign-in), `amr` / `aal` / `acr`, `enabled`. Configuration may lower what a provider's code declares, never raise it. `getFactors()` is what is available; `assess(factors)` what a set of satisfied factors amounts to, by NIST SP 800-63B: an email link never lifts a sign-in above AAL1, and only WebAuthn is phishing-resistant.
- __The session records how it signed in.__ Every sign-in path writes `req.session.signIn` (`signInRecord()`): the provider, each factor with its time, and `amr` / `aal` / `acr`. Step-up and UserInfo read it there. A delegated credential (agent token, share, app token) never starts a session and never satisfies step-up.
- __What is required is per role.__ Each role in `ngdpbase.roles.definitions` may carry `required-aal` (1–3). A person must meet the highest among their roles. `admin` and `user-admin` ship at 2; every other role at 1; `anonymous` has none.
- __A role above the sign-in steps down; nobody is locked out.__ In a session signed in below a role's level, the person acts without that role (`rolesAtSignIn`) and a banner says how to get it back. `profile-manage` and `account-security` stay, so the profile, where a passkey is added, is always reachable. When the level cannot be reached at all, see the next section.
- __Sensitive actions ask for a fresh sign-in.__ A permission entry marked `"step-up": true` needs a factor satisfied within `ngdpbase.auth.step-up.max-age-minutes` at the level the person's roles require. The PDP decides it inside `hasPermission`; a control's visibility asks `holdsPermission`, which leaves step-up out ([#1635](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1635)). Step-up itself is AuthManager's ([#1758](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1758)): the freshness rule (`stepUpNeeded`, which the PDP asks), the answer to a refused request (`askForFreshSignIn`, used by core routes and `ApiContext` alike), re-authentication (`completeReauth`) and every step-up record (`recordReauth`). An add-on marks its own permission the same way, in its `config/default-config.json`: permission definitions merge per entry, so it neither touches core's list nor another add-on's. The window is one site-wide value, shipped at 15 minutes; there is no per-action window, a site wanting "prove it now" sets 1, and 0 turns step-up off ([#1767](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1767)).
- __Every sign-in completes in one step:__ `AuthManager.completeSignIn(req, result, how)` ([#1758](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1758)). Password, passkey, second factor, email link and outside providers all end there: a new session (#1043), the identity, how it signed in (`session.signIn`), the password-change generation (#1482), the private stores (a handle already unlocked, or the password handed to the vault unlocker that the vault code registers with `setVaultUnlocker`), one audit record of the completed sign-in naming how, the count on the account, and a pending outside identity carried across the new session, never connected (#1743, below). `authenticate()` records failures and re-checks; a successful sign-in is recorded only here. A route saves the session and answers, going to the returned `next` when it is set (the page that asks whether a pending outside identity is the person's), nothing more. A new sign-in method calls `completeSignIn` with `returnTo` (where it was going) and honours `next`.
- __Turning an account off or deleting it goes through one step:__ `AuthManager.closeAccess(username, 'disable' | 'delete', ctx)`, called by `UserManager` ([#1765](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1765)). Both end every session. Disable leaves tokens, app grants and shares to be refused live (their owner is inactive) and keeps the credentials, so turning the account back on restores them. Delete revokes the agent tokens, app grants and shares, and removes the account's credentials-store rows: a row names its account by username, so one left behind would sign in to a new account later created with the same name. A new kind of access an account can hold is closed here, never by its own route.
- __Every way a session ends goes through one door:__ `endSession()` in `src/utils/sessionEnd.ts` drops the session's private-store keys and records `authentication-logout` with the reason ([#1670](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1670)).
- __A signed-in session ends at two limits, checked in one place__ ([#1767](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1767)). `ngdpbase.session.idle-timeout-minutes` (shipped 10080, 7 days; a role's `idle-timeout-minutes` may only shorten it, [#1546](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1546)) ends one with no request for that long; `ngdpbase.session.max-age` (shipped 2592000000 ms, 30 days) ends one that long after it signed in, however active. `checkSessionLimits` in `src/utils/sessionLimits.ts`, called by the session middleware in `app.ts`, decides both on every request, and the end goes through `endSession` with the reason `max-age` or `idle-timeout`. The cap counts from `session.signedInAt`, which only `completeSignIn` writes, on the new session; a re-authentication never moves it. The cookie and the file store roll forward on use (express-session re-issues the cookie whenever the session changes; the store expires a session after its last access), so they are never the cap: a sign-in sets its cookie to the cap so the cookie never ends a session sooner, and a session nobody signed in to lives a day. A new limit goes in `checkSessionLimits`, not in a route.
- __Sign-in throttling stays in memory__ (operator, 2026-10-11, [#1767](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1767)). `LoginThrottle` counts failures in each server process, so several copies of the server each allow their own `max-attempts`. At startup `throttlePerCopyWarning` logs one warning when pm2 runs more than one copy.
- __One email, one account__ ([#1748](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1748)). `UserManager` refuses to save an email another account already has, on create and on change, on every path (registration, profile, admin, outside sign-in). It's not a setting. Emails are compared trimmed and in any case, with no provider rules: `smiths+alice@gmail.com` and `smiths+bob@gmail.com` are different addresses. Accounts without an email are allowed. `getUserByEmail` returns nobody for an email two accounts already share (saved before the rule), and the startup check reports any such pair (manager degraded, log, admin notification) until an admin gives each account its own. The failure reason is `email-taken`; registration shows its safe message, and the profile page never names the other account.
- __An outside client's key is an agent token, never a shared key__ (operator, 2026-10-11, #1759). A person mints it, scoped to what the client needs; it is checked through AuthManager like any token, the request runs as that person within the token's scope, and deleting the token stops the client. Tokens arrive as `Authorization: Bearer`; the one exception is Dawarich, an Immich client, which sends its key in `x-api-key` on its two routes only (`isDawarichApiKeyPath`). To connect Dawarich: mint an agent token scoped to `media-read` on your profile and enter it as Dawarich's Immich API key (the old `ngdpbase.dawarichCompat.apiKey` setting is gone).
- __An outside sign-in signs in a connected identity, nothing else__ ([#1743](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1743), decided 2026-10-10). `OidcAuthProvider` is one class with one registered instance per enabled entry of `ngdpbase.auth.oidc.providers` (`oidc:<name>`; the old `ngdpbase.auth.google-oidc.*` keys are the `google` entry). The identity is issuer + subject, a credentials-store row of kind `identity` (subject `iss#sub`); each belongs to exactly one account, and it is a way in only while its provider is enabled. Email never matches or signs anyone in, groups and roles claims are never read, no access or refresh token is kept, and a refusal never says whether an account exists. Connecting is the signed-in person's own `account-security` change (`startConnect` → `connectIdentity`, step-up applies); disconnecting is `removeCredential` under the last-way-in rule. Every connection is made in one place, `attachIdentity`, and audited as a credential added with how it happened. Every outbound request (discovery, keys, the code exchange) goes through `guardedFetch`; a client secret, and Apple's signing key (`private-key`), are only environment references (`$NAME`). A provider with no `ngdpbase.auth.factors` entry is a primary method at AAL1.
- __A POSTed callback is relayed, never finished as a POST__ ([#1743](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1743) slice 4b). Apple returns with `response_mode=form_post`: a cross-site top-level POST, which carries none of this site's `SameSite=Lax` cookies, so neither the session nor the `ngdp_oauth_state` binding (#1630) is there. `src/middleware/outsideCallbackRelay.ts` is mounted in `app.ts` before the session and CSRF middleware, on `POST /auth/oauth/:name/callback` only, and only for a provider `AuthManager.callbackIsPosted` names (a preset with `responseMode: 'form_post'`). It answers with a 303 to the GET callback carrying `code`, `state` and `error`, nothing else; the GET brings the Lax cookies and the ordinary finish runs. Being before the session, it starts no session whose cookie would replace the person's own. Any other POST to a callback falls through to CSRF and is refused. Do not exempt a path in `csrf.ts`, and do not finish a sign-in on the POST: the browser binding is the protection against login CSRF. Apple's client secret is a JWT the provider signs per exchange (`SignedClientSecret`), not a shared secret.
- __An identity connected to no account goes to the email step, or gets an account__ ([#1743](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1743) slice 3, decided 2026-10-11). `AuthManager.finishOutsideSignIn` decides every outside callback; the route only answers. A verified identity that no account has connected is held in the session as a pending connection (provider, `iss#sub`, the verified email or `''`, the provider's name, 5 minutes; one at a time, a new one replaces the old), and the sign-in page says "Sign in to connect your *Provider* account (*email*)" with a way to cancel (`POST /login/connect/cancel`). __A sign-in never connects it by itself__ (review, 2026-10-11): on a shared computer the waiting identity may be someone else's, and connecting it would let them sign in as the next person. `completeSignIn` carries it across the new session and sends the person to `GET /login/connect/confirm`, which asks "Connect the *Provider* account (*email*) to your account *username*?" and warns that anyone who can sign in to it will be able to sign in here. Only Connect (a CSRF-protected POST) calls `confirmPendingConnection`, which checks again (signed in, not expired, provider enabled and allowed for the account, not connected elsewhere) and connects through `attachIdentity`. Don't connect drops it and goes where the sign-in was going; ignored, it expires; signing out drops it (`endSession`). Email never signs anyone in and is never looked up, so the page is the same whether or not an account has that email. An account is created instead only when `ngdpbase.auth.oidc.auto-provision`, the provider's own `auto-provision` and `ngdpbase.application.registration` are all true and the provider verified the email: by magic-link auto-provisioning's path (`deriveUsername`, `UserManager.createUser`, which records it; no password, registration's default roles, none from the provider), then the identity is connected and the person signed in through `completeSignIn`. The same rule, asked for every enabled provider, is `outsideSignUpOpen()`: the header's Register button counts it as open sign-up and leads to the sign-in page, where the provider buttons are. If the identity cannot be connected, the account just created is deleted through `UserManager.deleteUser` (which closes its access) and nobody is signed in, so no account is left with no way in. An email that already belongs to an account creates nothing: the email step, with registration's `email-taken` message.
- __The sign-in page labels each method; an outside provider's button has one rendering path__ ([#1781](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1781), operator, 2026-10-12). `views/login.ejs` shows a headed section per method that is on, in this order: Username and password, Passkey, External provider, with "— or —" only between sections shown. The passkey section starts hidden and `passkey.js` shows it (`data-passkey-section`) where the browser supports passkeys, so its "or" hides with it. Every outside provider's button, on the sign-in page and the profile's Connect, is drawn by `views/_outside-provider-button.ejs` from `OutsideProvider.icon`, which `resolveOutsideProviderIcon` resolves once at registration from the entry's `icon`: an `https://` image, a site path, or a Font Awesome name; anything else is refused with a warning and the default (by preset) shown. Views never decide a provider's icon by its name.
- __Passwords:__ scrypt at N=2^17, hashed off the request path (async), and re-hashed at the current cost on the next sign-in ([#1632](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1632)). The check is `PasswordAuthProvider`'s, through `AuthManager.authenticate`; `UserManager` keeps the stored hash and hands it only to that check ([#1758](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1758)). AuthManager audits every sign-in method's check itself, with where it came from (`AuthAttempt`), and counts a sign-in (not a re-check) on the account; routes write no audit record for a check. The minimum length is `ngdpbase.user.security.password-min-length` (6).

## When a role's level cannot be reached

Operator rule (2026-10-08): __whenever there is no way to reach AAL2, AAL1 must be set__ — and the person is told, until they fix it, what to add.

| Situation | What a password sign-in gets | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The site offers no AAL2 method (no explicit https `base-url`, so no passkeys), and the level is the shipped default | The roles, at AAL1. AuthManager reports `degraded` and says how to fix it | `effectiveLevels()`, [#448](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/448) |
| The site offers no AAL2 method, and the operator set the level in `app-custom-config.json` | The boot refuses and names the role | `checkRequiredAal()`, [#1523](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1523) |
| The account's `allowedAuthMethods` exclude every AAL2 method | The roles, at AAL1 | `aalReachFor()`, [#1690](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1690) |
| The account is allowed a passkey but has not enrolled one | The roles, at AAL1 — and a red banner on every page, which cannot be dismissed, saying to add a passkey, until one is | `rolesAwaitingEnrolment()`, [#1690](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1690) |
| The account has an AAL2 method it can use | Roles above AAL1 step down for this session; the banner offers the passkey sign-in | `rolesAtSignIn()`, [#448](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/448) |

`aalReachFor(username)` gives `{ now, ifEnrolled }`: what the account's allowed, offered and (for a passkey) enrolled factors reach, and what they would reach once enrolled — both at least 1. `now` is carried on the request subject as `aalCap` and caps the level everywhere it is enforced: `rolesAtSignIn`, `requiredAalFor`, `stepUpNeeded` and the re-auth page. A factor that has to be enrolled before it counts is listed in `ENROLLED_FACTORS` in `AuthManager.ts`.

## Checklist for a new sign-in provider or factor

1. Write it as an `AuthProvider` and register it with `AuthManager.registerProvider` (an add-on registers it at load). No route calls it directly.
2. Declare its `amr`, `aal` and `acr` honestly, by SP 800-63B. Configuration can lower them; nothing can raise them.
3. Add it to `ngdpbase.auth.factors` (shipped, or in the add-on's `config/default-config.json`), with `primary` set only if it can start a sign-in on its own.
4. If it must be enrolled before it counts (a key, an app), add it to `ENROLLED_FACTORS`, store its rows through the credentials store, and offer enrolment on the profile's sign-in methods.
5. Its sign-ins produce a `signInRecord`, and success and failure are audited (`authentication-success` / `authentication-failed`) through `AuditManager.logAuthentication`.
6. Tests: the assessed level with and without it; a role at AAL2 kept or stepped down as the table above says; the audit records written. Sabotage each once.

## How you know you are done

- `npm test -- src/managers/__tests__/AuthManager.test.ts src/managers/__tests__/AuthManager.completeSignIn.test.ts src/security/__tests__/PolicyDecisionPoint.stepUp.test.ts src/utils/__tests__/sessionEnd.test.ts src/utils/__tests__/sessionLimits.test.ts`

## Known gaps

- [#1767](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1767) — copies of the server a process cannot see (several containers or pods behind one address, several hosts) get no throttling warning; only pm2's are detected. A session signed in before #1767 starts its 30-day cap at its first request after the upgrade, and keeps its old one-day cookie until the person signs in again. The page warns before an idle sign-out, not before the cap ends a session
- [#1758](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1758) — paths that check a secret outside AuthManager: the profile password re-check and the login and store-door fallbacks; the password check moves into `PasswordAuthProvider`
- [#1745](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1745) — an add-on route that refuses a step-up permission answers a bare 403 (`ApiContext.requirePermission`), with no way to re-authenticate as core routes give
- [#1522](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1522) — multi-factor epic: TOTP ([#421](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/421)), device authorization ([#1526](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1526)), link-based second factor ([#1532](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1532))
- [#1743](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1743) — sign-in through any OpenID Connect provider. Built: the generic provider with issuer + subject sign-in, connecting and disconnecting identities on the profile (slices 1–2), and the email step (connected only on the person's confirmation after they sign in) and account creation (slice 3). Slice 4a: the `microsoft` preset (personal accounts only), the authentik operator guide, and the header's Register button counting outside account creation as open sign-up (`outsideSignUpOpen`, leading to the sign-in page). Slice 4b: the `apple` preset (a client secret signed per exchange from a key held as an environment reference, the POSTed callback relayed to the GET, `email_verified` as a string); built against Apple's published discovery document and documentation, not yet tried with a real Apple sign-in. Not yet: an admin connecting an identity on a person's behalf, notices to the person on connect / disconnect (#1699), throttling refused outside sign-ins, a warning when the identity's email differs from the account's, and `login_hint`. With account creation on, the email step's `email-taken` message tells the person (who proved that address to the provider) that an account has it; with it off nothing does. The decided design is in [planning/authentication.md](../planning/authentication.md#external-identity-providers-decided-2026-10-10)
- [#1545](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1545) — account recovery
- [#1594](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1594) — strong sign-ins unlock private stores
- [#1633](https://github.com/jwilleke/ngdpbase/issues/1633) — an outside provider's own second factor counts toward AAL2
